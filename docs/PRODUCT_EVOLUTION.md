# Democracys.org: from scores to public understanding

## North star

Help a person understand a democratic institution, investigate a claim with evidence, and take a safe, constructive next step. A growing page count or an increasingly precise-looking ranking is not the outcome.

## Shipped in this release — September 8, 2026

- Change explorer: exact two-year comparisons, all five tests, coverage exclusions, no causal or significance claims.
- Printable country briefs: sources, uncertainty, discussion questions, downloadable country CSV, no email gate. Print variants are noindex to avoid competing with full profiles.
- Toolkit: classroom exercise, reporting checks, institution questions, and a worksheet that does not submit or persist personal notes.
- Shareable three-country comparison URLs and smaller country data requests, with failure handling.
- Read-only JSON/CSV country endpoint for reuse with source and license metadata.
- Search discovery: descriptive links, canonical new tools, sitemap inclusion. Ownership and sitemap submission must be confirmed separately in Search Console; neither guarantees indexing or rankings.

## Highest-value next investments

1. **Accountability before authority claims.** Name a responsible editor, a working correction channel, funding and governance disclosures, and a public correction log. Do not invent a staff, advisory board, or inbox. Recruit independent methods and country reviewers; publish review scope and conflicts.
2. **An evidence layer beneath changes.** Add dated institutional events with links to primary records, clearly labeled interpretation, alternative explanations, and review dates. International IDEA's Democracy Tracker is useful context, not a license to copy its narratives or attribute precise composite points to events. This requires human review and source licensing checks.
3. **A reliable release system.** Version data, method and code independently; run reproducibility tests and missingness/bounds checks; publish a meaningful source-update changelog. Treat trend uncertainty as a methods research question, not something derivable by subtracting endpoint bounds.
4. **Teaching distribution.** Pilot the lab and briefs with teachers, libraries, and journalists. Observe task completion and understanding with consent. Improve from their feedback, then translate with qualified native-language review and appropriate country context.
5. **Useful partnerships and reuse.** Offer stable data contracts and documented, accessible chart embeds after external users validate the need. Approach relevant institutions with specific useful resources; no mass link schemes, paid ranking claims, or fake endorsements.
6. **Local democracy, carefully.** Validate a separate local-institution instrument with appropriate evidence and reviewers. The current worksheet must not turn subjective answers into a national-comparable score or publicly rank unverified local bodies.

## What to measure

- Whether readers distinguish a score from a percentage and an observation from a cause.
- Whether teachers/reporters can obtain a cited brief or reproducible dataset without help.
- Verified corrections resolved, review coverage, data freshness, and accessibility issues resolved.
- Search Console indexing, relevant queries and useful landing pages—not rankings promised in advance.
- Real reuse by educators, researchers, and public institutions; publicly verifiable citations, not invented reach.

Avoid collecting worksheet text or political profiles for analytics. Add any new telemetry only with an explicit data-minimization and privacy decision.
